## 18:00 · DSA A1 — heapq and bisect

### Cover

- `bisect`: binary search you should stop rewriting.
- 1–2 problems.

### Gist
A **binary heap** is a complete binary tree stored in a flat array: children of
`i` live at `2i+1` and `2i+2`, parent at `(i-1)//2`. No pointers, perfect cache
behaviour. The invariant is only *parent <= children* — a heap is **not**
sorted, and that weaker promise is exactly why push and pop are O(log n) rather
than O(n).

`heapq` is a **min-heap** with no max-heap mode. For a max-heap, push `-x`, or
push tuples `(-priority, item)`. Key facts: `heappush`/`heappop` O(log n),
`heapify` O(n) — not O(n log n), because most nodes are near the bottom and
sift down only a little — and `heap[0]` is a free peek. `nlargest(k, xs)` is
O(n log k), which beats sorting when `k << n`. `heapreplace` / `heappushpop`
are the one-pass versions; use them in a "keep the best k" loop.

`bisect_left` / `bisect_right` give you the insertion point in a **sorted**
sequence in O(log n) — `bisect_right(a, x) - bisect_left(a, x)` is the count of
`x`. `insort` inserts in sorted position, but the insert itself is O(n) shifting
(see Wednesday), so it is fine for small n and wrong for a hot loop. `key=` is
supported on modern Pythons.

The judgement call worth internalising: **heap when you want the extreme
repeatedly, sorted-array + bisect when you want order and range queries,
dict when you want membership.**

### Gist — the three things this chapter is really teaching
**1. Solution sets have structure.** The general solution of `Ax = b` is *one*
particular solution plus *anything* in the null space of `A`. So there are
exactly three outcomes: no solution, one solution, or infinitely many — never
two. Once you see it, under-determined systems stop being mysterious, and
"infinitely many solutions, pick the nice one" is exactly what regularisation
does later.

**2. A basis is a coordinate system, and it is a choice.** Same vector, same
map, different numbers, depending on the basis you wrote them in. Change of
basis (§2.7.2) is `B^-1 A B` — conjugation — and when you hit PCA, eigenbases
and SVD in a few weeks, all of them are "find the basis where this operator is
boring." Getting this now is worth two weeks later.

**3. Rank is the honest measure of information.** `rank(A) = dim(image)`. Full
rank means nothing is lost. Rank deficiency means columns are redundant — which
in data terms is correlated features, and is why your matrix is singular and
your inverse blows up. Row rank equals column rank; that is not obvious and it
is worth sitting with.
