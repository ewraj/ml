### Gist
**Amortized ≠ average.** Average case is over random *inputs*; amortized is a
worst-case guarantee over a *sequence* of operations. A `list.append` is O(n)
whenever it reallocates, but the reallocations are rare enough that any n
appends together cost O(n) — so O(1) amortized.

Why: doubling means copies happen at sizes 1, 2, 4, 8, … n. Total elements
copied is `1 + 2 + 4 + … + n < 2n`. Linear total, constant per op. Growth by a
*constant* (`capacity += 8`) instead of a *factor* gives 8+16+24+… = O(n²).
The factor is the whole trick. CPython's actual growth is ~1.125x plus a
constant, not 2x — still geometric.

### Gist — the three rules that do 90% of the work

**1. Broadcasting.** Align shapes from the *right*. Each dimension pair must be
equal or one of them must be 1; a 1 is stretched. Missing leading dims are
treated as 1. Nothing is copied — the stretched axis is given stride 0, so it
reads the same memory over and over. That's why `(n,1) + (1,m) -> (n,m)` is
cheap to set up and allocates only the result.

**2. `keepdims=True`.** A reduction eats an axis: `X.sum(axis=1)` on `(n,d)`
gives `(n,)`, which broadcasts against the *columns* — wrong. `keepdims=True`
gives `(n,1)`, which broadcasts back against the rows — right. Nearly every
per-row normalization bug is this.

**3. `einsum`.** Name every axis, repeat a name to contract it, list the output
axes. `"ik,kj->ij"` is matmul; `"bik,bkj->bij"` is batched; `"ii->i"` is the
diagonal; dropping a name from the output sums over it.

### The ten functions

Two are the real session, the rest are warm-up:

- **`pairwise_sq_dists`** — expand `‖x−y‖² = ‖x‖² + ‖y‖² − 2x·y`. Three pieces

- **`softmax`** — subtract the row max before `exp`. Not cosmetic:
  `exp(1000)` overflows to `inf` and you get `nan`. Subtracting the max leaves
  the result mathematically identical and the largest exponent at `exp(0)=1`.
  Then `keepdims` twice.
