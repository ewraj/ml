### Gist — this is Monday's `pairwise_sq_dists`, grown up
The whole algorithm is: *distances -> k smallest -> majority vote.* There is no
training. `fit` stores the data; all the cost is at predict time, which makes
k-NN the cleanest possible illustration of the bias/variance and
memory/compute trade-offs you will meet all phase.

**Distances, vectorized:**
- **L2 (squared).** `‖x-y‖² = ‖x‖² + ‖y‖² − 2·x·y` -> `(n,1) + (1,m) − 2·X@Y.T`.
  One BLAS matmul does the real work. Do **not** take the square root: it is
  monotonic, so it cannot change the ranking, and skipping it saves an `n·m`
  pass. Clip tiny negatives to 0 — the expansion is not exactly non-negative in
  floating point, and a `-1e-13` becomes `nan` the moment you do take a root.
- **L1.** `np.abs(X[:, None, :] - Y[None, :, :]).sum(-1)` is correct and
  materialises an `(n, m, d)` array — for MNIST that is the memory wall you
  will hit today. Chunk over rows of `X`. There is no matmul trick for L1;
  that asymmetry is itself the lesson.
- **Cosine.** L2-normalize both sides (Monday's `row_l2_normalize`), then
  cosine *distance* is `1 − Xn @ Yn.T`. Guard zero rows before dividing.

**Picking k neighbours:** `np.argsort` sorts all m — O(m log m) per row and
wasteful. `np.argpartition(D, k, axis=1)[:, :k]` is O(m), then sort just those
k. On 10k test x 60k train that difference is minutes.

**Voting:** gather the neighbour labels, then `np.bincount` per row (or a
one-hot sum / `np.take_along_axis` for the fully vectorized version) and
`argmax`. Decide the tie rule deliberately — lowest label wins, or nearest
neighbour among the tied — and write down which you chose. Distance weighting
(`1/d`) is the natural extension; watch out for `d == 0`.

**Memory is the real engineering problem today.** A full `(10000, 60000)`
float64 distance matrix is ~4.8 GB. Process test points in chunks of a few
hundred rows and accumulate the top-k. Doing this yourself is the actual
systems content of the session.

### Watch for
- `float32` vs `float64` — half the memory, and the expansion trick loses more
  precision. Know which you chose and why.
- Never touch the test set while choosing k. Split a validation set off train.
- The `(n, m, d)` broadcast in L1 is seductive and will kill the kernel at
  MNIST scale. Chunk first, do not wait for the crash.

## 20:00 · Trading T1.1 — What a market actually is

**T1: how markets work.** No strategies, no charts, no indicators for three
months. Tonight: map the Indian market end to end and **write down who actually
holds your shares.**

### Cover
NSE / BSE · SEBI · depositories (NSDL, CDSL) · brokers · clearing corporations.
Then: what happens between clicking buy and owning something.

### Gist — the chain, and the questions to answer yourself
Answer each in your own words. Where a rule may have changed recently, check
the primary source (SEBI circulars, NSE/BSE and NSDL/CDSL sites) rather than a
blog — settlement timelines in India have moved more than once.

1. **Exchange.** NSE and BSE run the order books. What kind of matching —
   price-time priority? What is the tick size, what are the trading hours, and
   what is a circuit limit?
2. **Broker.** Your order goes to the exchange through a member broker. What
   does the broker actually hold on your behalf, and what does it not?
3. **Matching.** Your buy meets someone's sell in the matching engine. At that
   moment, what do you own — shares, or a contractual claim?
4. **Clearing corporation.** NSE Clearing / Indian Clearing Corporation steps
   in between buyer and seller (**novation**) and becomes the counterparty to
   both. Why does that exist? What risk is it eating, and what does it demand
   in return (margin)?
5. **Settlement.** Money and securities actually change hands on the settlement
   cycle. **Look up the current cycle and what it applies to** — this has
   changed in recent years. What is the difference between trade date and
   settlement date, and what is a settlement guarantee fund?
6. **Depository.** NSDL / CDSL hold shares in dematerialised form. Your demat
   account is with a **depository participant**; the depository is the record.
   So: is your name on the company's register? What is a beneficiary owner
   account?
7. **Paper trail.** Contract note, CAS from the depository, ledger from the
   broker. What is each, and which one do you trust if two disagree?

### The question the session exists for
> Most people trade for years without knowing this: **if your broker vanished
> overnight, where would your shares be, and who could prove they are yours?**
